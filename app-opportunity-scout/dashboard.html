What this is and isn't. Every number comes from Apple's public endpoints or is calculated from them with a formula you can read. Search volume, keyword difficulty and revenue are not shown at all — they need a paid provider or unobservable inputs, so they are marked UNAVAILABLE rather than estimated. Downloads appear only as a wide range built on one assumption you control. App order is iTunes Search API relevance, not the live App Store ranking. Rating count is a proxy for competitor traction — not difficulty, downloads or demand.

Reading the table. Low competition is not opportunity. The question is “can relatively small or new apps rank here?” — which is why the Top-10 ladder is shown raw. A big incumbent at #1 does not disqualify a keyword: if 95- and 420-rating apps hold slots beside it, the door is open. Equally, a field of four tiny apps is not automatically good — it may just have no demand. Under-500 is one signal, never a requirement. A keyword with zero competition and zero demand scores zero here by design — the opportunity score multiplies its four factors, so a weak one is never averaged away.
